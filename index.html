Five ways to fall out

MODE 01

Race

Three laps, no rules worth mentioning. Handbrake into the corner, come out sideways, take somebody's rear bumper with you.

MODE 02

Sumo

An orange ring, a bottomless pit in the middle and five people who used to be your friends. Last car in the ring takes the round.

MODE 03

Bomb Tag

A live bomb, a very short fuse and one car holding it. Bump somebody to pass it on — whoever’s still carrying when it blows is out. Rounds run until someone hits the lobby’s score.

MODE 04

Track Builder

Snap pieces together in the desert or the snowfield, drop the start line and race it thirty seconds later. Saved tracks stay in the rotation.

MODE 05

Turf

The arena gets carved into slices, one per car, painted to match. Shove the ball into yours and the points tick up for every second it stays there — which is never as long as you’d like, with five other bumpers on their way over.

The arsenal

One slot. Eight bad ideas.

You carry exactly one power-up at a time. Driving through a crate is therefore a decision, not a freebie — whatever you were saving for the last corner is gone the moment you touch a new one.

The developer

{{ devName }}

CrashFest is a one-person game, built in Unity between coffee and complaints from playtesters. It started as a physics experiment about shoving a low-poly car off a ledge and got out of hand once six controllers were plugged in.

I’m a software engineer, not an artist. So a good chunk of CrashFest was made together with Claude AI — art direction, shaders, menus, the visual bits I’d never have drawn on my own — and the soundtrack comes out of Suno. The code, the physics and every questionable design decision are mine.

Bug reports and terrible track designs are equally welcome.
